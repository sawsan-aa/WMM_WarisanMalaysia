ABOUT MALAYSIA

Nestled at the crossroads of Southeast Asia, Malaysia is a land of breathtaking contrasts, where ancient rainforests meet gleaming skylines, and centuries-old traditions thrive alongside bold modernity. From the towering twin spires of Kuala Lumpur to the mist-shrouded longhouses of Sarawak, every corner whispers a story of resilience, craft, and cultural pride. Three great civilisations, Malay, Chinese, and Indian, have shaped this nation into a living tapestry of colour, flavour, and spirit.

SPIRITUAL SPACES

Sites of culture and history, where the sacred and the everyday meet in a living synthesis of faith, tradition, and community

BATU CAVES

Gombak, Selangor

A giant Hindu statue stands at the foot of a rainbow staircase leading to temples set inside limestone caves. It remains one of the most recognisable pilgrimage sites just beyond Kuala Lumpur.

THEAN HOU TEMPLE

Robson Heights, Kuala Lumpur

An ornate temple dedicated to Thean Hou, the Queen of Heaven and protector of those who make their living from the sea. Its architecture borrows freely from Buddhist, Taoist and Confucian traditions.

PUTRA MOSQUE

Putrajaya, Federal Territory

Masjid Putra is the principal mosque at the heart of Putrajaya. Calm, welcoming and serene, its architecture blends modern design with traditional Islamic art, crowned by a rose-pink dome mirrored in the still water below.

Architectural devotion carved in stone, steel, and ancient timber across a land shaped by many civilisations

PENINSULAR REGION

Petronas Twin Towers

"Icons of Modern Malaysia"

Standing 452 metres tall, the Petronas Twin Towers were once the tallest buildings in the world and remain Kuala Lumpur's defining skyline. Designed by César Pelli and inspired by Islamic geometry.
