Cultural Etiquette

A respectful traveler is a welcomed guest. Malaysia is a tapestry of traditions; here is your guide to navigating our customs with grace and warmth.

01. Dress Code

Modesty is highly valued, especially when visiting religious sites. Loose, breathable fabrics are your best friend in our tropical climate.

PRO TIP

Always carry a light sarong or scarf in your bag for unexpected temple visits.

DO

Dress Modestly

Cover shoulders and knees when visiting mosques, temples, or rural villages. It shows deep respect for local values.

RESPECTFUL

DON'T

Wear Skimpy Clothing

Avoid short shorts, sleeveless tops, or swimwear outside of beach resorts and private pools to avoid causing unintended offense.

OFFENSIVE

02. Greetings

Malaysians are warm and hospitable. How you acknowledge others sets the tone for your entire interaction.

LOCAL LINGO

Saying "Terima Kasih" (Thank You) with a smile will open many doors and hearts.

DO

The 'Salam'

A gentle touch of both hands and bringing them to your heart is the traditional Malay greeting. A polite nod and smile works universally.

HEARTFELT

DON'T

Initiate Contact

Avoid initiating handshakes with the opposite gender unless they offer first. Some may prefer a polite bow for religious reasons.

BOUNDARY

03. Dining

Food is the soul of Malaysia. Sharing a meal is a sacred act of community, and knowing the rules makes it even more delicious.

CULTURAL FACT

In traditional homes, water is often brought in a 'kendi' to wash hands at the table.

DO

Use Your Right Hand

Always use your right hand for eating, passing food, or giving/receiving items. The left hand is traditionally considered unhygienic.

TRADITIONAL

DON'T

Point With Fingers

Never point at people or food with your index finger. Use your thumb with fingers folded, or a polite open-palm gesture instead.

IMPOLITE
